The Interview / p.12

Forty players, one tempo, and a deploy on Sunday

recorded over two long blacks, twice interrupted by a rehearsal call
John Vincent Caballero · Cebu, Philippines · five years in · answers lightly edited

Q  You direct a marching band and you ship software. Which is harder?

The band, easily. You cannot hotfix a field show at halftime. But they teach each other — both are forty small decisions that only work if everyone hits the same beat, and both fall apart the moment you stop rehearsing.

Q  Everyone wants AI in their product. What do you tell them?

That most of what they want is an if-statement with good manners. I'll happily build the model part — after we've found the boring 80% that doesn't need one. Clients think that's me talking them out of work. It's the opposite: it's what makes the model part survive contact with reality.

Q  Full-stack, mobile, automation — isn't that spread thin?

It's one skill wearing three coats: I like owning the thing from the first conversation to the deploy. Small teams don't need a specialist for each layer, they need someone who won't hand off the awkward middle.

Q  A bar-gig setlist saved a launch?

Sort of. I built Setlist between two acoustic sets because I kept forgetting capo positions. The offline sync pattern I hacked together that night is exactly what shipped in the clinic app six months later.

Q  Gacha games, manga, gadget reviews. Do those show up in the work?

Constantly. Gacha UI is the most ruthlessly optimised interface design on earth — every tap is measured, every reward is paced. I've stolen more from a pull animation than from most dashboards. And reviewing gadgets keeps me writing about things I use, which is the same muscle as writing a case study.

* he did in fact check his phone for the pull rates mid-answer. twice.
